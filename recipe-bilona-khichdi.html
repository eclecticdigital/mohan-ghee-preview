Bilona
Khichdi.

बिलोना खिचड़ी

The way Dadi made it — moong dal and rice, slow-cooked into one warm pot, finished with a generous dollop of bilona ghee. Easy on the stomach, heavy on memory.

15Min Prep
25Min Cook
3-4Servings
EasyDifficulty

What You'll Need

Ingredients.

  • The Base
  • ½ cupYellow moong dal (split, washed)
  • ¾ cupBasmati rice
  • 5 cupsWater
  • 1 tspSalt (or to taste)
  • ½ tspTurmeric powder
  • For the Tadka
  • 2 tbspMohan Ghee® Cow Ghee
  • 1 tspCumin seeds (jeera)
  • A pinchHing (asafoetida)
  • 1Dried red chilli, broken (optional)
  • 1 inchGinger, julienned
  • 8-10Curry leaves
  • ¼ tspBlack pepper, freshly cracked
  • To Finish
  • 1 tbspMohan Ghee® Cow Ghee — for the dollop
  • 2 tbspFresh coriander, chopped
How to Make

The Method.

  1. Wash and soak.

    Wash the moong dal and rice together in cold water until the water runs almost clear. Soak in fresh water for 15 minutes. Drain.

  2. Pressure cook.

    In a pressure cooker, add the soaked rice and dal, 5 cups of water, salt, and turmeric. Stir gently. Close the lid. Cook on medium-high for 1 whistle, then on low flame for 2 more whistles. Switch off. Let the pressure release naturally — about 10 minutes.

  3. Open and check.

    Open the cooker. The khichdi should be soft and porridge-like, not too dry, not soupy. If it's too thick, add ½ cup hot water and stir. The texture should be comforting, easy to spoon — what Ayurveda calls "sukha-snigdha" (dry-yet-moist).

  4. Heat the tadka ghee.

    In a small tadka pan, melt 2 tbsp Mohan Ghee on medium flame. When the ghee shimmers and gives off its sweet, nutty aroma, lower the flame. The fragrance is the first half of the dish.

  5. Bloom the spices.

    Add cumin seeds first — they will crackle and turn golden in 5 seconds. Quickly add hing, broken red chilli, julienned ginger, and curry leaves. The curry leaves will splutter and release their oil. Add cracked black pepper.

  6. Pour the tadka over.

    Immediately pour the sizzling tadka over the warm khichdi. The aroma will hit first — earthy, spicy, ghee-rich. Stir gently to mix. Cover for 2 minutes so the flavours marry.

  7. Serve with the dollop.

    Spoon into a steel katori or thali. Place 1 tablespoon of fresh Mohan Ghee right on top — it will melt slowly into the warm khichdi. Sprinkle fresh coriander. Serve with papad, kachumber salad, and a wedge of lemon.

★

Grandmother's Tip

Khichdi is the dish you make when someone is unwell, when the weather turns, when the rest of the world feels too noisy. Don't skimp on the final dollop of ghee — that's not garnish, that's the medicine. A teaspoon of ghee makes the whole dish digestible and healing, the way Ayurveda has known for 5,000 years.
