Aloo Puri
Thali.

आलू पूरी की थाली

A festival morning, finished with a generous spoon of Mohan Ghee. Pillowy puris fried golden, paired with a slow-spiced potato curry — the way grandmother made it.

20Min Prep
30Min Cook
4Servings
EasyDifficulty
SLOW-COOKED
IN GHEE
SINCE 1947
What You'll Need

Ingredients.

  • For the Puris
  • 2 cupsWhole wheat flour (atta)
  • ½ tspSalt
  • 1 tspMohan Ghee® Desi Ghee, melted
  • ¾ cupWarm water (approx)
  • For fryingMohan Ghee® Desi Ghee — about 500 ml
  • For the Aloo Curry
  • 4 mediumPotatoes, boiled and cubed
  • 2 tbspMohan Ghee® Desi Ghee
  • 1 tspCumin seeds (jeera)
  • 1Onion, finely chopped
  • 2Tomatoes, puréed
  • 1 tspGinger-garlic paste
  • 1 tspTurmeric powder (haldi)
  • 1½ tspCoriander powder (dhania)
  • ½ tspRed chilli powder
  • 1 tspGaram masala
  • To tasteSalt
  • 2 tbspFresh coriander, chopped
How to Make

The Method.

  1. Make the dough.

    In a wide bowl, combine the wheat flour and salt. Add the melted ghee and rub it into the flour with your fingertips until it resembles breadcrumbs. Slowly add warm water and knead for 5–6 minutes until you get a firm but supple dough. Cover with a damp cloth and rest for 15 minutes.

  2. Start the curry.

    Heat 2 tbsp of Mohan Ghee in a heavy-bottomed kadai over medium flame. When the ghee shimmers, add the cumin seeds. Wait for them to crackle — this is the moment that defines the dish.

  3. Build the masala.

    Add the chopped onion and sauté until golden. Add ginger-garlic paste and cook for 30 seconds. Pour in the tomato purée and cook until the ghee separates from the masala — about 6–8 minutes. This is the slow building of flavour.

  4. Add the spices.

    Stir in turmeric, coriander powder, red chilli powder and salt. Cook for one more minute. Add the boiled potato cubes and gently fold so each piece is coated. Add ½ cup hot water, cover, and simmer on low for 8 minutes.

  5. Finish the curry.

    Sprinkle garam masala and most of the chopped coriander. Switch off the flame. Let it rest, covered, while you fry the puris.

  6. Roll and fry the puris.

    Divide the rested dough into 12 small balls. Roll each into a 4-inch round, slightly thicker than a roti. Heat fresh ghee in a kadai for frying. To test: drop a small piece of dough — it should rise immediately. Slide a puri into the ghee. Press gently with a slotted spoon — it will puff like a balloon. Fry for 30 seconds, flip, fry another 20 seconds till golden. Drain on paper.

  7. Serve hot.

    Plate the puris with the aloo curry. Top with remaining coriander. Add a tiny dollop of ghee on top of the curry just before serving — that's the touch that makes it home.

★

Grandmother's Tip

The puris will tell you when they're ready — they balloon up the moment the ghee is hot enough. If they sink and stay flat, the ghee isn't ready. If they brown too fast, lower the flame. Trust the puri, not the timer.
