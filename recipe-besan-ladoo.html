Slow-roasted gram flour, drowned in pure ghee, rolled in your palm. The smell that hits you the moment Diwali begins — and the one your hands will smell of for hours.

What You'll Need

Ingredients.

  • The Base
  • 2 cupsCoarse besan (gram flour) — preferably ladoo besan
  • ¾ cupMohan Ghee® Desi Ghee — generous and melted
  • 1 cupBoora / powdered sugar (tagar), sifted
  • To Finish
  • ½ tspGreen cardamom powder (elaichi)
  • 2 tbspAlmonds, slivered
  • 2 tbspCashews, broken
  • 1 tbspPistachios, slivered
  • A pinchSaffron strands (optional, for kesari ladoo)
  • For Garnishing
  • 2 tbspPistachio slivers
  • 14Whole almonds (one for each ladoo, optional)
How to Make

The Method.

  1. Warm the ghee.

    In a heavy, non-stick kadai, melt ¾ cup of Mohan Ghee on low flame. Don't rush this — the ghee should be warm but not smoking. The aroma will fill the kitchen first.

  2. Add the besan.

    Add the coarse besan into the warm ghee. Stir continuously with a wooden spatula. The besan will look loose and pasty at first — keep moving it on low flame.

  3. Roast — slow, patient, steady.

    This is the soul of the recipe. Roast on the lowest flame for 20–25 minutes, stirring constantly. You'll watch the besan turn from pale yellow → deep golden → reddish-gold. The aroma will sweeten and deepen. Don't walk away — burnt besan is bitter and ruined.

  4. Smell the ready signal.

    You'll know it's done when the besan smells nutty and warm, the colour is deep gold-brown, and the ghee has separated and risen to the top — leaving the besan loose, almost foamy. Switch off the flame.

  5. Add the chopped nuts.

    Stir in the slivered almonds, broken cashews and pistachios. The residual heat of the ghee will toast them lightly. Let the mixture rest for 5–7 minutes until it's just warm to the touch (but not cold — sugar can't go in a cold mixture).

  6. Add the boora and cardamom.

    Sift in the boora (powdered sugar) and cardamom powder. Mix gently with the spatula until everything comes together. If using saffron, crush it between fingers and add now. The mixture should hold its shape when you press it — if it crumbles, drizzle a teaspoon more melted ghee.

  7. Roll while warm.

    Take a small portion (about 2 tablespoons) and roll between your palms into a smooth round ball. Press a single almond on top, or roll in pistachio slivers. Place on a plate. Repeat for all 14 ladoos. They will firm up as they cool.

  8. Store and savour.

    Once fully cool, store in an airtight steel dabba. They keep beautifully for 10 days at room temperature, longer if refrigerated. The flavour matures by day 2 — patience rewards you.

★

Grandmother's Tip

The lowest flame, the longest patience. Most ladoos fail because the besan was roasted on too-high heat — it browns on the outside but stays raw underneath, and the ladoo crumbles. The flame should barely flicker, the spatula should never stop moving. 25 minutes is normal. Half an hour is even better.
